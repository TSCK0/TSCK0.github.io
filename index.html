Garuda Mark 1: coaxial swirl injector

Garuda Mark 1 is a pressure-fed liquid bipropellant engine, gaseous oxygen and ethanol, designed for 400 N of thrust. I worked on the injector design and analysis, and on the electronics and test stand.

How the numbers were reached

  1. Mission. A 5 kg payload to 5 km with a maximum wet mass of 30 kg gave a velocity requirement of 575.4 m/s, including a 1.3 factor for drag and other losses.
  2. Propellants. Gaseous oxygen and ethanol, chosen for cost, handling and availability.
  3. Combustion analysis. A NASA CEA sweep over chamber pressures of 3 to 10 bar and ratios of 0.5 to 2 settled on 10 bar and 1.0.
  4. Thrust. 400 N gives a thrust-to-weight ratio of 1.6 for a conservative 25 kg rocket.
  5. Injector. Orifice areas from Huzel and Huang, then swirl chamber geometry following Xue, Jog and Jeng (AIAA Journal, 2004).
  6. Iteration. Three CAD versions in OnShape and Fusion 360, with fluid flow simulated in SimScale.
